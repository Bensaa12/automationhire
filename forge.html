How It Works

From photo to CAD file
in three steps

No modelling skills, no software install. Upload, describe, download.

01
📸
Upload a photo or describe your component
Drag in a photo of any real component — a valve, pump, skid, fitting, enclosure — or simply describe it in words. The more detail you give, the better the geometry.
02
⚙️
AI generates parametric 3D geometry
PartForge uses Claude vision AI to extract dimensions and geometry from your photo, then Articraft generates a parametric CadQuery model compiled against OpenCascade OCCT — producing true B-Rep solids, not just meshes.
03
📦
Preview interactively, download your files
Review the articulated 3D model in your browser with joint controls. Then download STEP for Plant 3D / SolidWorks / Inventor, URDF for digital twins, or OBJ/STL for visualisation and 3D printing.

FAQ

Common questions

PartForge generates STEP (ISO-10303-21), URDF, OBJ, and STL. Pro users also receive an AutoCAD Plant 3D .pcat catalog entry. STEP files use true B-Rep solid geometry via the OpenCascade OCCT 7.8 kernel — not tessellated meshes — so they import cleanly into Plant 3D, SolidWorks, Inventor, Catia, and FreeCAD.
Accuracy depends on the quality of your input. With a clear photo and a description that includes key dimensions (e.g. "DN50 bore, 200 mm face-to-face"), the model will match within 2–5%. For free-tier text-only generation, proportions are realistic but not to exact engineering tolerances. For critical engineering use, always verify against manufacturer datasheets before use in production design.
Simple single-part components take 5–10 minutes. Complex multi-part assemblies (like a full hydraulic skid) take 20–45 minutes. Pro tier uses high-quality multi-turn AI reasoning for best results. Premium uses medium thinking. Free uses fast mode. You'll receive an email notification and can also poll the live progress bar in the browser.
PartForge is built on Articraft, an open-source agentic 3D generation system, combined with CadQuery (a Python CAD API over OpenCascade) and Claude AI (Anthropic) for geometry reasoning. The pipeline: Claude vision extracts geometry from your photo → Articraft generates a parametric Python model → CadQuery compiles it to B-Rep solids → exported as STEP/URDF/OBJ/STL.
Yes. Premium and Pro tiers support assemblies. You can load the URDF in Gazebo, ROS, or any robotics simulation environment, or use the STEP assembly in your CAD package.
